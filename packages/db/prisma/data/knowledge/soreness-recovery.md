- Delayed onset muscle soreness (DOMS) usually starts 12–24 hours after unfamiliar or harder exercise, peaks around 24–72 hours, and fades within a few days. It lessens as the body adapts and is not a measure of a good workout.
- Light activity (walking, easy cycling) often eases soreness. Stretching before or after exercise does not meaningfully reduce soreness (Cochrane 2011). Foam rolling may feel good, with small effects.
- Training a sore muscle lightly is usually fine; severe soreness, swelling, or dark urine after extreme exercise needs a doctor.
- Recovery depends mostly on sleep, enough food and protein, and sensible training load. A lighter week every 4–8 weeks (a deload) can help when fatigue builds.
- Persistent fatigue, falling performance, poor sleep, low mood and frequent illness together can mean too much training and too little recovery; reduce load and review food and sleep.
