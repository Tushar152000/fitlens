- Food first: supplements only help once diet, training and sleep are in place (IOC 2018).
- Good evidence for performance: creatine and caffeine (see their notes). Protein powder is just convenient food.
- Correcting a deficiency (vitamin D, iron, vitamin B12, common in vegetarians) helps health and performance, but should follow a blood test and a doctor's advice; iron in particular is harmful in excess.
- BCAAs add nothing when daily protein is adequate. "Fat burners" and "testosterone boosters" have poor evidence and higher contamination risk.
- Supplements can be contaminated with banned or harmful substances (IOC 2018). Prefer third-party tested products; competitive athletes must be especially careful.
- Never recommend anabolic steroids, SARMs or prescription drugs.
