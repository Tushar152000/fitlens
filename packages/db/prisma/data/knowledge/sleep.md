- Adults need 7 or more hours of sleep a night regularly (AASM/SRS 2015); 7–9 hours is the recommended range for adults 18–64 (NSF 2015).
- Short sleep hurts training quality, recovery and appetite control. In one controlled study, dieters sleeping 5.5 hours lost a smaller share of their weight as fat and more as lean mass than when sleeping 8.5 hours (Nedeltcheva 2010).
- Helpful habits: a regular sleep and wake time, a dark, cool and quiet room, no caffeine late in the day, a lighter meal close to bedtime, and less screen time before bed.
- Ongoing insomnia, loud snoring with pauses in breathing, or daytime sleepiness need a doctor.
