- Beginners do well with run-walk intervals (for example 1 minute running, 2 minutes walking), 3 days a week, building up over 6–10 weeks to continuous running.
- Increase running load gradually. In novice runners, increasing weekly distance by more than about 30% over two weeks was linked with more injuries (Nielsen 2014). The popular "10% rule" is a conservative guide, not a proven limit.
- Keep at least one rest or easy day between harder runs at first, and include strength training for legs and hips.
- Choose shoes that feel comfortable; comfort is a reasonable guide, and no shoe type reliably prevents injury (Nigg 2015).
- Pain that changes how you run, gets worse during a run, or lasts into the next day needs rest from running and, if it persists beyond a week or two, a physiotherapist.
