- Sedentary adults: the Indian requirement (ICMR-NIN 2020) is about 0.83 g per kg body weight a day. That prevents deficiency; it is not the amount that best supports training.
- People who exercise: 1.4–2.0 g/kg/day (ISSN). For muscle gain with resistance training, the benefit levels off around 1.6 g/kg/day on average, and up to about 2.2 g/kg covers almost everyone (Morton 2018).
- In a calorie deficit, higher intakes help keep muscle: about 2.3–3.1 g per kg of lean mass for lean, trained people (Helms 2014; ISSN). For most app users, 1.6–2.2 g/kg of body weight is a practical range while losing fat.
- Spread it over 3–5 meals of roughly 0.25–0.4 g/kg each (about 20–40 g), every 3–4 hours (ISSN).
- Total daily protein matters most; timing and powder vs food matter much less.
- High-protein diets show no harm to kidneys in healthy people. Anyone with kidney disease must follow their doctor's protein advice.
- The user's own protein target comes from the app; use the context or tools, not this range, when talking about their numbers.
